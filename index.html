01 · The problem

You run the routes.
The chasing runs your month.

If you collect from three hundred homes across a few colonies with two or three collectors, nobody handed you an accounts department. Your system is a paper diary, a bundle of loose receipts, and your own memory — and all three quietly break down.

📓

The diary can't tell you who's behind

By the 15th you're flipping pages to reconstruct which homes on which lane still owe. Some get asked twice, some never get asked at all.

🚶

The collector re-walks lanes for nothing

Without a route-by-route collection view, your worker knocks on doors that already paid and skips ones that didn't. Time and diesel, gone.

🧾

"Bhaiya, receipt do na"

Homes that paid want proof. You're tearing a slip from a book or promising a receipt later — and later never quite comes, so disputes linger.

💸

Small dues leak into real money

Rs 100 a home feels trivial to chase — until forty homes carry two months of arrears and it's the collector's whole salary sitting uncollected.

02 · The insight

The collection fee is the most predictable recurring payment there is — same homes, same amount, same day — yet it's still chased on foot, lane by lane, from a diary.

SafaiBook's whole job is to close that gap: take a thing that repeats perfectly and let it run as a cycle — generate the dues, send the reminders, issue the receipt — so collection happens whether or not you remembered to start it.

03 · How it works

Set it up once. Then every month runs itself.

  1. 1

    Register homes across your routes

    Each route has a worker; each home gets its monthly fee and due day. Enter it once — that's the whole setup for a full colony.

  2. 2

    On the due day, invoices generate

    SafaiBook raises one collection invoice per active household and rolls any carried-forward arrears into each home's balance. Re-running never duplicates.

  3. 3

    Staged reminders go out on WhatsApp

    A nudge 3 days before, a note on the due day, a firmer one if it goes overdue — each drafted to the household, deduped so no home is ever pinged twice for the same stage.

  4. 4

    Payment clears, numbered receipt goes back

    Record cash or UPI and the due clears while a numbered digital receipt is issued automatically. Your dashboard always shows collected vs pending, by route.

07 · Who it's for & questions

Built for the private collection operator, not the municipal corporation.

SafaiBook is for the person who runs door-to-door waste collection as a small business — two or three collectors, a few colonies, Rs 80–150 a home each month. Big enough that chasing the fee eats your month; small enough that municipal ERP software is overkill and out of reach. If you're running it on a paper diary today, this is for you.

Private collection operators RWA-appointed collectors Sanitation contractors 2–3 collectors · a few colonies
Does it actually send messages, or just remind me to?

SafaiBook drafts each reminder — personalised, with the household name, route, month and balance — and stages it in your outbox on schedule. You send from your own number with a tap, so it always looks like it came from you, not a robot. And it never double-sends the same stage to the same home.

Households pay by cash and UPI. Does that work?

Yes. Mark a cash payment as received and the due clears and a numbered receipt goes out, same as UPI. SafaiBook tracks each home's balance — it doesn't force a single payment rail on the colony.

I run three collectors across a few colonies. Can I see it by route?

That's the core view. Every household sits on a route with a worker, and the dashboard shows collection percentage route by route — so you know which lane your collector still needs to work before month-end.

What does it cost?

Pricing is worked out on a quick discovery call and billed monthly on subscription. We're keeping public prices off the page while we onboard our first operators.
